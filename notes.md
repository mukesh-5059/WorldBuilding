  Here is a curated list of the best articles, open-source projects, academic papers, and video series for learning procedural world generation using tectonic plates:
  ──────
  ### 🌐 1. Top Devblogs & Foundational Guides

  • Red Blob Games — Polygonal Map Generation https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/mapgen2/ (by Amit Patel)
      • Why Read: The absolute gold standard tutorial on generating world maps using Voronoi graphs, plate tectonics, elevation propagation, and biomes.
  • Procedural Planet Generation Series https://experilous.com/1/blog/post/procedural-planet-generation (by Andy Gainey / Experilous)
      • Why Read: A deep-dive 3D spherical planet generation breakdown. Explains 3D plate seeding, Voronoi dual grids, kinetic plate movement, boundary stress classification, and
      climate.
  • Generating Fantasy Maps https://mapterrain.blogspot.com/ (by Martin O'Leary)
      • Why Read: Excellent breakdown of plate dynamics, mountain building along boundary fronts, and hydraulic drainage networks.

  ### 💻 2. Landmark Open-Source Codebases & Demos

  • Tectonics.js https://davidson16807.github.io/tectonics.js/ (by Justin Kunimune)
      • Features: A full 3D interactive web application simulating plate tectonics on a sphere.
      • What You'll Learn: Euler rotation poles, subduction, crustal density, oceanic crust aging, thermal subsidence, and atmospheric circulation.
      • Source Code: GitHub - davidson16807/tectonics.js https://github.com/davidson16807/tectonics.js
  • WorldEngine https://github.com/Mindwerks/worldengine (by Federico Tomassetti)
      • Features: Open-source Python/C++ procedural world generator.
      • What You'll Learn: Tectonic plate growth algorithms, elevation fields, wind direction, rainfall shadows, and biomes.

  ### 📄 3. Academic Papers & Graphics Research

  • "Interactive Modeling of Tectonic Plates" https://hal.inria.fr/hal-01339224/document (Cortial et al., Eurographics)
      • Key Takeaway: A computer graphics paper detailing how to represent tectonic plates as rigid spherical polygons with velocity vectors to generate realistic mountain ranges
      and ocean trenches.
  • "Synthesis of Terrains directly from Tectonic Vector Fields" https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/ (Parry et al.)
      • Key Takeaway: Explains how to map boundary stress vectors (compression/extension) into continuous elevation heightmaps using distance fields and noise.
